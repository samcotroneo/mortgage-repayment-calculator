# Ledge design direction

## Visual world

Ledge is a calm, editorial money workspace: warm paper surfaces, ink-black typography, deep moss for trustworthy positive movement, and clay for attention. It should feel closer to a well-made financial journal than a bank portal. The visual language is confident through proportion, rules, and clear type rather than gradients, glossy cards, or generic fintech blues.

## Tokens

- Paper: `#f4f0e7`
- Paper elevated: `#fbfaf6`
- Ink: `#1f2b2d`
- Ink muted: `#66716d`
- Moss: `#5d735b`
- Moss pale: `#dfe8da`
- Clay: `#c8644c`
- Clay pale: `#f3ddd3`
- Sky pale: `#dce7e8`
- Rule: `#d9d8cf`
- Display type: Iowan Old Style, Baskerville, Georgia, serif
- Interface type: Avenir Next, Helvetica Neue, Arial, sans-serif

## Composition

The authenticated shell uses a persistent dark rail on desktop and a compact top bar on mobile. The overview opens with a single wide forecast surface, then a ledger-like account relationship list and a focused next-action panel. Content is grouped by information hierarchy, not by a wall of same-sized cards.

## Interaction

Charts and numbers must carry labels and plain-language interpretation. Every imported value exposes freshness. Upload and review are inline workflows with visible progress, validation, correction, and reversible commit states.

## Anti-goals

- No gradient text, decorative glass, or excessive pill controls.
- No dashboard made entirely from equal metric cards.
- No visual treatment that implies a forecast is a promise.
